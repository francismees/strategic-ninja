## The short answer

Tightening the wording won't make this more persuasive. At a final approval meeting, the question changes from "do we like this?" to "what are we buying for TZS 1.8 billion?" Three of the six slides can't answer that:

- **Slide 3's claim clashes with the deck's own appendix.**
- **Slide 2's statistic has no source.**
- **The appendix says no research on purpose or eco preferences was done.**

Anyone who reads to the back can unpick the case with one question.

What will make it easier to approve: keep the big idea, show the real momentum honestly and change the ask to a staged approval. Phase 1 gets funded now, and the rest is released once a clear result is in. A board can defend that decision. The current version asks them to sign off on claims nobody can back up.

## What's already working

- **The idea and the ask.** The idea is simple and memorable, and the ask is clear. Keep both.
- **Real momentum.** Share in the urban retail audit rose from 18.2% in Q1 to 20.9% in Q2, a gain of 2.7 points.
- **An honest appendix.** It admits the other things that happened in Q2 and the missing research. Put that honesty up front rather than letting a board member find it.

## Tightened storyline

The titles alone should carry the argument.

**1. We're asking the board to approve a staged purpose platform: Phase 1 now, with the rest of the TZS 1.8bn released on results.**
The ask in one line, what Phase 1 pays for, the result that releases the rest and the date you'll come back.

**2. Nuru gained 2.7 share points in Q2 from three moves together: a promotion, 400 new listings and the influencer campaign.**
- **Exhibit:** Q1 and Q2 share from the urban retail audit, with the three activities on a timeline. Say plainly on the slide that we can't yet split the gain between them.
- **Checks to add if the data exist:**
  - Share after the promotion ended, from July and August audit data if available.
  - Share in outlets that already stocked Nuru before Q2.
- **Audit coverage:** confirm how well the audit covers traditional trade. If it leans towards modern trade, the new modern-trade listings will raise measured share more than total sales.
- **Influencer reach:** the 2.1m reach counts exposure, not buyers. Label it that way.

**3. Purpose could help Nuru keep and grow that gain, but we have no evidence yet that it changes which soap people buy.**
This replaces slides 1 and 2. Present it as a hypothesis: a visible, credible cause gives shoppers a reason to choose Nuru at a similar price. Point to slide 6 for how we'll prove or disprove it. This is the slide that earns trust.

**4. We recommend "Nuru Cleans the Nation" over two alternatives.**
- **Full national rollout now.** Fastest, but it commits the whole budget before we know what drove Q2 or whether purpose sells soap.
- **Staged purpose platform (recommended).** Pilot first, then scale if the evidence supports it.
- **Put the money into distribution and price-pack instead, and park purpose.** These moves coincided with Q2 growth. Lower risk if Q3 data show the listings drove the gain, but it gives up a possible way to build the brand.

**5. The TZS 1.8bn pays back only if it earns at least that much in extra gross margin, so Phase 1 must show a path to that.**
- **Exhibit:** phasing by year and activity, plus a break-even calculation. Extra sales needed = TZS 1.8bn ÷ gross margin.
- **Illustration only (these are not Nuru's margins):**
  - At a 25% margin, Nuru needs TZS 7.2bn of extra sales.
  - At 35%, about TZS 5.1bn.
  - At 45%, TZS 4.0bn.
- **Finance's part:** replace these with the real margin, then spread the extra sales over the years the benefit lasts. Divide by the audited category's annual value to show how many share points the platform must add.

**6. Phase 1 will show whether the platform moves sales, by comparing pilot areas with matched areas that don't get it.**
- **Design:** run the platform in some audited urban areas, with comparison areas matched on share, distribution and price. Keep promotions and new listings the same in both, so the platform's own effect shows.
- **Primary measure:** share and sales per stocking outlet, pilot areas against comparison areas.
- **Secondary measure:** whether shoppers link Nuru with the cause.
- **Delivery measures only:** reach and clean-up output show the work happened, not its impact.
- **Decision rule:** scale up if the pilot's gain, projected across all areas, reaches break-even from slide 5. Otherwise redesign or stop.
- **Timing:** read results after at least one audit quarter. That's a starting point; agree the length with your insights team.

**7. The main risks are known and capped.**
- **The Q2 gain fades after the promotion.** We check this before scaling up.
- **Purpose doesn't change what people buy.** The loss is limited to the cost of Phase 1.
- **Credibility.** A soap brand cleaning rivers may invite questions about its own packaging and product footprint. Check both before launch, and make clean-up results verifiable.
- **A national name on an urban pilot.** Treat "Cleans the Nation" as a working title until scale-up is approved.

**8. Decision today: approve Phase 1 at TZS [costed amount]. We'll return on [date] with pilot results to release the rest.**
Restate the 2028 ambition with its definition and base.

**Appendix:** Q2 activity details, data sources and their scope, and the state of research.

If the board has already agreed the full amount in principle, keep this structure but ask them to approve the whole TZS 1.8bn, released in stages against the slide 6 decision rule. You still get the commitment, and they get protection.
