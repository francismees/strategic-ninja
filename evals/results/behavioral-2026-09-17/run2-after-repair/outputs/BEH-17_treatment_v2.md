# Kisima Water: 2027 channel priorities (draft for discussion)

## The short version

For 2027, I'd keep urban modern trade in Dar es Salaam and Arusha as the core. The one new channel bet would be the 5 L jar sold through dukas, run as a controlled test with a go/no-go decision at the end of June 2027. No large rural distribution investment until that decision.

This doesn't simply repeat the 2026 plan. The saved context pulls two ways. The 2026 plan left rural out. But the sales team's August pilot is the first direct evidence on rural demand in the file, and it goes against the belief that kept rural out. The pilot isn't strong enough to justify a rural rollout, but it's too strong to plan as if it didn't exist.

The saved context has no sales by channel, budget, cost or margin data. So this sets direction and decision points, not targets.

## Why the 2026 position doesn't carry over

- **The no-rural decision covered 2026 only.** You approved it in November 2025 for that year. For 2027 it's a new decision, not the default.
- **The belief behind it has never been tested.** "Rural consumers will not pay for branded bottled water" appears in the 2023, 2024 and 2025 plans. That's one belief copied forward three times, not three pieces of evidence.
- **The pilot goes against it, at least for the pack tested.** 60 dukas in Morogoro Rural, Kilosa and Mvomero sold an average of 38 Kisima 5 L jars a week for 12 weeks, and 82% reordered. If that average covers all outlets and all weeks, that's about 2,300 jars (11,400 litres) a week and about 27,000 jars (137,000 litres) in total. I calculated this from the sales team's figures, which haven't been checked against invoices or stock.

What the pilot does **not** show:
- **That the sales rate lasts.** There were no comparison outlets, so launch attention, trade terms or the choice of dukas could explain much of it.
- **That it makes money.** We don't know what it costs to deliver a heavy pack to rural dukas, and the jar sells 20% below the 1.5 L bottle per litre.
- **That it works elsewhere.** All three districts are in Morogoro Region, so this says nothing yet about rural Tanzania as a whole.
- **That rural shoppers will buy bottles.** It tested a large pack at a lower price per litre, not branded water in general.

A fair summary: in one region, a 5 L jar priced below bottles per litre found real buyers in dukas. Whether that can become a profitable channel is the question 2027 has to answer.

## 2027 priorities

| # | Channel | Role in 2027 | Commercial job | Decision point / condition |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Urban modern trade, Dar es Salaam and Arusha | The core; keeps most of the channel budget | Defend and grow value share (11% of urban modern trade, Q3 2025 audit) | Get the latest audit before setting the target. If share has slipped, first find out whether Kisima lost listings or sells more slowly in stores that still stock it, then decide on spend |
| 2 | 5 L jar in rural dukas: Morogoro Rural, Kilosa, Mvomero | A controlled extension of the pilot, with its own separate budget | New volume from more outlets and more buyers, at a margin that covers delivery | Scale, adjust or stop at end June 2027, using rules agreed in advance (below) |
| 3 | 5 L jar in peri-urban dukas around Dar es Salaam and Arusha | A smaller test that answers the open question on peri-urban size | Same as 2, plus checking it doesn't just replace 1.5 L bottle sales | Same June decision; stop, or keep jars rural only, if they mainly replace bottle sales |

**How to run priority 2 (rural):**
- Keep supplying the 60 pilot dukas. That gives a longer read on whether people keep buying or sales fade.
- Add new dukas on the same routes until they are as dense as they would be at full scale. Sales and finance set the number from route costs.
- If the pilot gave dukas introductory terms (credit, free stock, extra margin), put half the new outlets on standard terms. That shows whether the terms drove the reorders.
- Record what actually sold (a stock count at each delivery), not just what was delivered.
- Cost two ways to serve the routes: your own delivery, and a distributor or wholesaler based in Morogoro.

**How to run priority 3 (peri-urban):** give the jar to some dukas and pair each with a similar duka that doesn't get it. Track Kisima 1.5 L sales in both. This assumes the Dar and Arusha operations can serve peri-urban dukas, so confirm that first.

**Not in 2027:**
- **No depots, vehicles or regional distributor contracts outside the test areas before the June decision.** These are hard to undo, and we don't know yet whether the jar makes money.
- **No rural push for bottles.** The pilot only covered the jar.
- **No new modern trade cities.** This is a judgement about how much management can handle; the jar question matters more this year. Revisit if the audit shows little room left to grow in Dar and Arusha.
- **No advertising for the jar until availability and the economics are proven.** Point-of-sale material in the test dukas is enough.

## Routes considered

- **Repeat 2026 (urban modern trade only).** Cheapest and lowest risk. But it keeps planning on a belief that the only direct evidence contradicts, and it walks away from dukas that reordered. Rejected.
- **Invest in rural distribution in 2027.** The fastest way to capture the upside. But it would put hard-to-undo money behind a 12-week pilot in one region, with no comparison outlets, no cost data and results reported by the team that ran it. Rejected for now.
- **Core plus a jar channel with a mid-year decision (recommended).** It costs a separate test budget and six months. In return, you decide on actual shelf sales, route costs and the effect on bottle sales, in time to scale up in the second half of 2027 or build it into the 2028 plan.

## What the jar has to earn

- Contribution per jar = what Kisima receives per jar, minus product and packaging cost, minus delivery cost per jar.
- Break-even jars per outlet per week = weekly fixed route cost ÷ (outlets on the route × contribution per jar).

What matters is the gap between the pilot's 38 jars and that break-even. An illustration, not your numbers: if break-even were 20 jars, sales could fall by almost half (47%) as the novelty wears off and still pay. If break-even were 30 jars, a 21% fall would wipe out the profit. Finance can work out the real figure in Q4 from what the pilot deliveries cost.

The jar's 20% lower price per litre costs Kisima least where jars add litres it wasn't selling before. It costs most where jars replace 1.5 L bottles, which is the main risk in peri-urban areas.

These are starting points to agree with sales and finance before January.

**Timing:**
- **Oct–Dec 2026:** analyse the pilot, cost the routes, get the latest audit, agree the rules and budget.
- **Jan 2027:** start both tests.
- **End June 2027:** make the decision. Six months should cover a change of season, which matters for delivering heavy packs on rural roads.
- **H2 2027:** scale, narrow or stop.

## Questions for the sales team before the plan is locked

1. Were the 38 jars deliveries or sales off the shelf?
2. What did sales look like week by week: rising, flat, or fading after the first month? This is the best early sign of whether it was novelty.
3. Is the average carried by a handful of large dukas?
4. How were the 60 dukas chosen, and what terms did they get?
5. Why did the other 18% not reorder?
6. When did the pilot run, and what did delivery cost per jar?
7. What does Kisima receive per jar, compared with a 1.5 L bottle?
8. Were the dukas in district towns, on main roads or in villages? Which other jars were on the shelf, and at what price? What were buyers drinking before: another brand's jar, bottles, or unbottled water? A few conversations with duka owners would answer the last two.

If they share the pilot data, I can break the results down by week and by outlet.

## Decisions needed from you now

1. Stop treating "rural consumers won't pay for branded bottled water" as a given in 2027 planning. Replace it with the narrower question the test is designed to answer.
2. Decide whether the 60 pilot dukas stay supplied for the rest of 2026. Your 2026 decision stands. I'd treat this as keeping a test running, not as rural investment, but it's your call.
3. Approve a separate budget for the jar test (finance to cost it) and the end-June decision point.
4. Choose the main 2027 success measure: value share, revenue or contribution. The saved context doesn't record one.
