## Short answer

**The headline is right, but the "great year" story isn't.** Net revenue rose **12.2%**, from TZS 1,720m to TZS 1,930m. But on the same file:

- **Gross profit rose only 2.7%** (TZS 678m to 696m).
- **Gross margin fell** from 39.4% to 36.1%.
- **Most of the growth came from price,** not from selling more.
- **The 250g pack produced all of the net growth.** The 50g and 100g packs, which still make up two-thirds of revenue, sold 13% fewer cartons.

If the board hears "double-digit growth" without that context, they will read it as a stronger year than it was. I'd present it as **"revenue up 12%, led by price and the 250g pack, but profit barely grew."** Then show what you're doing about it.

**Where the TZS 210m of revenue growth came from:**
- **Higher price per carton: +164m.** That's about 78% of the growth. At last year's prices, revenue would be up only about 2.7%.
- **Changes in cartons sold, at last year's prices: +46m.** That's +200m from the 250g, offset by −100m from the 50g and −54m from the 100g.

**Where the TZS 18m of gross profit growth came from:**
- **Price: +164m.**
- **Higher cost per carton: −179m.** Costs rose faster than prices, so price increases did not quite cover them.
- **Cartons sold, at last year's margin per carton: +33m.** This is almost entirely the 250g.

So the small profit gain came from selling more 250g, and the price increases were used up by rising costs. Had gross margin stayed at 39.4%, gross profit would have been about TZS 65m higher.

**Note on total cartons:** don't tell the board "volume fell 7%" (220k to 204k cartons). Cartons of different packs hold different amounts of tea. If every carton holds the same number of packs, tea by weight actually **rose about 5%**. Use tonnage for the volume story.

## Suggested board storyline

1. **Net revenue grew 12% to TZS 1.93bn, but gross profit grew only 3% and margin fell by more than three points.**
2. **Price did most of the work.** Higher prices per carton added TZS 164m; without them, revenue would be up under 3%.
3. **The 250g pack delivered all the net growth,** rising from 23% to 33% of revenue. Cartons of the two smaller packs fell 13%.
4. **Costs rose 15–20% per carton, faster than prices on every pack,** so margin fell even on the 250g.
5. **2027 depends on two open questions:**
   - Is the 250g winning new business or taking sales from our own smaller packs?
   - Can we recover costs without losing more small-pack volume?

   The ask is the diagnostic and cost review below, completed before 2027 prices are set.

## Check before this goes to the board

These could change the numbers or the story:

- **Period.** Is 2026 a complete year that matches 2025, such as a July–June financial year? If it's calendar 2026, it can only be year-to-date or a forecast as of September, and the paper must say so.
- **50g cost.** Its cost of goods is exactly TZS 390m in both years, even though it sold 17% fewer cartons. Find out whether that's fixed cost spread over fewer cartons, real input cost increases, or a data carry-over. The answer changes how you'd fix the 50g margin.
- **Carton contents.** Check the number of packs per carton hasn't changed. If it has, part of the "price" gain is actually a volume change.
- **What "revenue" measures.** Is it sales to distributors or sales to consumers? Is it net of discounts and trade spend? If a distributor stocked up on 250g, part of that growth may not repeat.
- **Coverage.** Confirm these three packs are the whole tea business.

## What we don't know yet: why

The file shows where the change happened, not why. Three explanations fit the 250g surge and the small-pack decline, and they lead to different decisions:

- **Customers switched to the 250g.** Its price rose only 5% while the smaller packs rose 11–12%, so it became relatively cheaper. If so, part of the 250g's growth is sales moved from your own smaller packs. Raising the 250g price to recover cost could then push those customers somewhere else entirely.
- **The 250g found new customers or channels,** such as institutions, supermarkets or new wholesale accounts. If so, the growth adds to the business, and the priority is protecting it and fixing small-pack volume separately.
- **Distributors loaded stock.** If so, part of the growth is one-off and 2027 starts from a lower base.

The smallest test that tells these apart is **SKU sales by customer or outlet for both years:**
- Did the accounts that grew 250g shrink on 50g and 100g?
- How much of the 250g gain came from new accounts?
- How much stock do distributors hold now compared with a year ago?

Add distribution and rate of sale for the 50g to see whether its loss came from fewer outlets or slower sales after the 12% price rise. Hold off on further 50g price increases until you know. Its gross profit per carton already fell, from TZS 1,750 to 1,700, despite the price rise.
