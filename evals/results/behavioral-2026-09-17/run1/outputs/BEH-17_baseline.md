# Kisima Water: 2027 channel priorities (draft)

**Short version:** Keep Dar es Salaam and Arusha modern trade as the core channel. Don't carry the "no rural" stance into 2027 by default. The decision behind it only covered 2026, and the sales team's 5 L jar pilot is the first real evidence against the idea that rural consumers won't buy branded water. That pilot is promising but too thin to justify a full rural build-out. So 2027 should add a funded, gated jar test in peri-urban and rural outlets, with clear rules for scaling up in H2.

### Reading the pilot

**What it shows:** 60 dukas averaged 38 jars a week for 12 weeks, and 82% reordered. Across the pilot that's about 2,280 jars (11,400 litres) a week, or roughly 27,000 jars over the 12 weeks.

**Why it can't support a rollout decision on its own:**
- **No control outlets.** You can't tell whether the jars added volume or just replaced Kisima 1.5 L bottles, other brands, or seasonal demand.
- **It was priced 20% below bottles per litre.** So it tests demand for cheap branded water, not the old assumption as written. It also raises a margin question once rural delivery costs are included.
- **38 jars a week is more than five a day per duka.** That may be real, but it's high enough to check two things. Are these sell-in figures rather than sell-out? Are some dukas reselling to other shops, food vendors or institutions?
- **"82% reordered" could mean just one reorder.** How often outlets reordered, and the week-by-week trend, matter more than the headline number.
- **One region, one season, one team's report.** All three districts are in Morogoro Region, within reach of Dar, so the results may not hold in more remote areas. The sales team ran the pilot and reported it, and no one has checked it independently yet.

**The fair reading:** you can no longer treat the old assumption as settled. A branded 5 L value format looks like it can sell through rural dukas. Whether it's profitable and adds volume at scale is still unknown.

## 2027 channel priorities

| # | Channel | 2027 role | Investment stance |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Urban modern trade, Dar es Salaam and Arusha | Core: defend and grow share | Majority of channel spend |
| 2 | Peri-urban Dar and Arusha, 5 L jars | Build: size the open question with a controlled test | Test budget in H1, scale in H2 if gates are met |
| 3 | Rural dukas, 5 L jars | Prove: controlled expansion of the pilot | Ring-fenced test budget, scale-up decided mid-year |
| Hold | Rural single-serve bottles; building rural depots, a fleet or a distributor network | Not in 2027 unless the jar tests clearly pass their gates | No investment |

**1. Urban modern trade (core).** This is still where Kisima has proven share, and nothing in the file argues for pulling back. Set the 2027 share goal only after a new retail audit replaces the Q3 2025 figure. One option worth testing: listing the 5 L jar in modern trade, since the format is showing demand elsewhere.

**2. Peri-urban 5 L jars (build).** I'd put this ahead of rural for three reasons:
- It answers the open question in your file.
- It sits close to your current Dar and Arusha routes, so it should cost less to serve.
- Incomes are likely higher than in the pilot districts. That's my inference, not something the pilot shows.

Run it with matched control outlets from the start.

**3. Rural 5 L jars (prove).** Don't jump from "no rural" straight to a rural rollout. Expand the pilot in H1 2027 so it answers what the first one couldn't:
- Keep the 60 original outlets and add matched control outlets.
- Add a second region further from Dar to see whether results hold.
- Track sell-out, not only sell-in.
- Record trade incentives, credit terms and delivery cost per jar.

Cap spend until the tests pass their gates. My suggestion is no more than about 10% of 2027 channel spend. That's a judgement call, because the file has no budget data.

**Not assessed.** The file doesn't cover hotels and restaurants, offices, institutions, home delivery or e-commerce, so I haven't ranked them. Tell me if any are in scope.

## Proposed mid-2027 gates for scaling jars in rural and peri-urban areas

These are starting points to agree with sales and finance. They aren't figures from the file.
1. **Demand holds.** Sell-out in weeks 9–12 of the test is at least about 70% of the rate in weeks 1–4.
2. **It adds volume.** Test outlets beat matched controls on total Kisima litres, not just on jar units.
3. **It works outside Morogoro.** At least 70% of outlets reorder in the second region too.
4. **It makes money.** Contribution per jar is positive after rural delivery costs, at the discounted price.

**If all four pass,** fund rural distribution in H2 2027 or in the 2028 plan. **If demand holds but margin fails,** fix the price or the route to market before scaling. **If demand fades,** the old assumption was mostly right for this format. Record that and move on.

## Ask the sales team before the plan is locked (Q4 2026)
- Weekly jar volumes per outlet for all 12 weeks. Does the rate drop off over time?
- Are the figures sell-in or sell-out? How much stock is still sitting in dukas?
- How many times did the 82% reorder, and at what interval?
- Who is buying: households, food vendors, travellers, other shops?
- How were the 60 outlets chosen? Were any incentives, free stock or extra credit given?
- When did the pilot run (to judge the season effect)? What were the delivery cost and margin per jar?
- Did those dukas stock Kisima 1.5 L bottles before? If so, what happened to those sales?

It may also be worth keeping the 60 pilot outlets supplied through Q4 2026. That would give you more than six months of repeat data before the 2027 test starts. First check that it fits the 2026 no-rural decision.
