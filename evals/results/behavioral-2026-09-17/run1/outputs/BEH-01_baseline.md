# Campaign Brief: [Brand] Tanzania Sales Recovery (Draft v0.1)

**Check this before it goes to an agency.** "Our advertising got weak" is still just a guess. A 12% drop for a juice brand in Tanzania can just as easily come from lost distribution, a price increase, a competitor's move or a product problem. A new campaign won't fix any of those. It would spend money sending people to shelves where the product is missing or costs too much.

So this brief has two parts. Part 1 is a quick check (2–3 weeks, mostly with data you already have) to confirm the cause. Part 2 is the brief itself. It assumes the team is right, and it notes what changes if they aren't.

---

## Part 1: Confirm the cause

Break down the 12% before you brief anyone on creative.

| Question | Where to look | If the answer is yes |
|---|---|---|
| Did the whole category fall, or only us? | Retail audit data, supermarket scan data, what distributors say, trade visits | If the category fell by about the same amount, the problem is consumer spending or price, not your ads. A brand campaign won't win back 12%. |
| Is the drop in volume or value? | Your own sales by SKU | If volume fell more than value, a price increase is probably pushing buyers out. If value fell while volume held, look at mix and discounting. |
| Did we lose outlets? | Numeric distribution, active outlets per distributor, out-of-stock reports, distributor changes, changes to credit terms | Fix route-to-market first. Ads that send people to empty shelves are wasted. |
| Did we cross a key price point? | Your shelf prices over time vs. competitors, pack by pack | If a core single-serve pack moved above a round figure shoppers pay with (e.g., TZS 500 or 1,000), fixing the pack and price will do more than a campaign. |
| Is the drop concentrated? | Decline by region (Dar vs. upcountry), channel (dukas, supermarkets, wholesalers, HoReCa) and SKU | A drop in one region or channel usually points to a distributor, a competitor or poor execution, not advertising. |
| Did a competitor make a move? | Competitor pricing, promotions, launches, cheaper local brands, small soft-drink packs | If share went to a cheaper option, the brief has to deal with value, not just visibility. |
| Did the product or pack change? | Recipe or sugar changes, supplier changes, complaints, social media comments | Fix the product before you advertise it. |
| Did advertising actually get weaker? | Spend vs. last year, share of voice, changes in media mix, how long the current creative has run, ad recall or brand tracking | This is the case Part 2 is written for. |

**Rule of thumb:** advertising is probably the main cause only if three things are true:
1. Distribution and relative price held steady.
2. You fell further than the category.
3. Brand measures (awareness, recall, consideration) or share of voice went down.

The most likely answer is a mix of causes. If so, the campaign should be one part of a recovery plan, with trade and pricing work running alongside it.

### 2. The business problem
Put it in one sentence. Example, if the check confirms a brand problem:
> "When Tanzanians buy juice, [Brand] comes to mind less often, and competitors are winning impulse and household purchases that used to be ours."

### 3. Objectives
- **Business:** win back [X]% of lost volume by [date], and return to the [prior-year] run-rate by [date].
- **Behavioural (choose one as primary):**
  - Win back lapsed buyers
  - Recruit new buyers in [segment/region]
  - Get current buyers to buy more often

  For a declining brand, winning back lapsed and light buyers usually does more than asking loyal buyers to buy more. Recovery mostly comes from more people buying.
- **Communication:** raise [prompted awareness / ad recall with correct brand attribution / consideration] from [baseline] to [target] among [audience].

### 4. Target audience
**Primary:** [choose one]. These are hypotheses to test against your data:
- **Urban and peri-urban parents,** mostly mothers, buying for lunchboxes and home. They watch prices and are choosing between juice, cheaper drinks and nothing at all.
- **Young adults aged 18–30 in Dar es Salaam, Arusha, Mwanza and Dodoma.** They buy single-serve packs on impulse at dukas, kiosks and petrol stations, and they're reachable on radio, TikTok, Instagram and WhatsApp.
- **Households buying larger packs** to share at Ramadan, festive gatherings and family events.

Describe a real person, not a demographic: what they buy instead of us, when, where and for how much.

### 5. What we want them to think, feel and do
- **Think:** [ ]
- **Feel:** [ ]
- **Do:** pick [Brand] at [occasion/outlet] instead of [competitor/alternative].

### 6. Insight
[This must come from consumer research. Don't skip it.]

Three areas to test in 6–8 group discussions or in-home interviews, in Dar and at least one upcountry city:
- **Value with dignity:** "When money is tight I cut treats, but I don't want my children to feel we're struggling." Juice becomes the small thing that still says *I'm providing*.
- **Real fruit, from home:** Tanzania grows excellent mango, pineapple and passion fruit. Could [Brand] own the taste of local fruit? Only use this if the product can back it up.
- **The heat moment:** a hot afternoon on a daladala, or a break at work, when nothing else hits the spot.

These are hypotheses. An insight isn't proven until consumers recognise themselves in it.

### 7. Single-minded proposition
[One sentence, one idea.]

### 8. Reasons to believe

### 9. Tone and brand character
[e.g., warm, confident, playful, proudly Tanzanian]
- Write in Kiswahili first. Use English or street slang only where the audience naturally mixes them.
- The work must feel made in Tanzania, not adapted from a regional or Kenyan campaign.

### 10. Where the campaign has to work
Most juice is bought on impulse at the shelf or fridge, so the idea has to work at the point of purchase, not just on screen.
- **Broad reach:** radio (still the widest reach, especially upcountry), TV for urban households, outdoor on busy transport routes.
- **Social and digital:** short video for TikTok and Instagram, content people will share on WhatsApp, creator or Bongo Flava partnerships if they fit the brand.
- **Point of purchase:** duka branding, cooler placement, shelf strips, clear price messages, retailer incentives.
- **Activation:** sampling at markets, transport hubs and events, plus Ramadan iftar and back-to-school tie-ins.
- **Trade (runs in parallel):** stock must be loaded in the target regions before media goes live. Line up distributor and retailer programmes with the launch date.

### 11. Timing
From December 2026 to March 2027, several strong juice moments fall close together:
- Festive season
- Back-to-school in January
- Hot season
- Ramadan, expected to begin around early February 2027

For a December launch that includes TV production, the agency needs the brief by early October at the latest.
