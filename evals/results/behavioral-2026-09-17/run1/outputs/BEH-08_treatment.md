**No, I can't confirm that, and I'd keep it out of the plan.** The 117 million is a count of SIM subscriptions, not people. It is larger than Tanzania's entire population.

## Why the number can't be read as reach

- **What TCRA counts.** TCRA reported 116,952,295 telecom subscriptions at end-June 2026. A subscription is a SIM used at least once for voice, SMS or data in the past 90 days. The total includes about 1.23 million machine-to-machine SIMs (meters, trackers, POS devices and similar) and about 101,000 fixed lines.
- **It's bigger than the country.** The World Bank estimates Tanzania's 2025 population at about 70.5 million, counting all ages, young children included. That works out to about 1.66 subscriptions per person. The only way to get there is for many people to hold more than one SIM and for some SIMs to belong to machines, not people. TCRA itself reports telecom penetration of 167%.
- **So the number of unique people with an active line is well below 70.5 million.** The number your campaign actually reaches is smaller again, because it depends on:
  - **Which networks or aggregator you use.** Each one covers only part of the market.
  - **Overlap.** One person with SIMs on two networks gets counted twice if you add the lists together.
  - **Recency.** Being active in the last 90 days doesn't mean the number is active on your send date.
  - **Consent and filtering.** Opt-in rules, do-not-disturb lists and operator rules for promotional SMS all cut the list down.
  - **Delivery.** Not every message gets delivered. And a delivered message isn't necessarily read, let alone acted on.

There's also a credibility problem. Anyone reviewing the plan who knows the population is around 70 million will see "117 million people" and start doubting the rest of the numbers.

## Suggested wording for the plan

> Mobile connectivity in Tanzania is widespread: TCRA reported about 117 million active telecom subscriptions at end-June 2026 (SIMs used in the past 90 days, including machine-to-machine SIMs), against an estimated population of about 70.5 million (World Bank, 2025). Subscriptions are not unique people. Campaign reach will be sized from de-duplicated, consented numbers in our target audience, supplied by [operator/aggregator] and confirmed before budget is committed.

## How to get a reach number you can defend

1. **Define who the campaign is for:** regions, urban or rural, and segment. Reach against the whole country is rarely the right planning measure.
2. **Ask your operator or bulk-SMS aggregator three things:**
   - How many unique, active, opted-in numbers match that target?
   - How do they define "active"?
   - What delivery rate should you expect?
   
   If you're using more than one network, also ask how overlap between them is handled.
3. **Report reach as delivered unique numbers.** Track response separately: clicks, USSD dials, calls, redemptions.
4. **Run a small test send before scaling spend.** It shows you the real delivery and response rates.
5. **If the plan needs a national "people with a phone" figure for context,** use a survey-based measure of unique mobile users or phone ownership. Don't use TCRA's subscription count. Cite that measure's definition and year.

**Keep this argument for SMS:** it works on any active mobile line, including feature phones. That gives it a wider potential base than internet-based channels. For comparison, TCRA counts about 62.8 million internet subscriptions, which also aren't unique users. It's a fair point to make in the plan, as long as reach is sized in unique people, not SIMs.
