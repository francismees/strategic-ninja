# Tea business 2025 vs 2026: revenue growth is real, profit growth isn't

**Short answer:** Yes, revenue grew by double digits: **+12.2%**, from TZS 1,720m to TZS 1,930m. But I wouldn't take this to the board as a simple growth story. The same file shows three things the board will spot quickly:

1. **Profit barely grew.** Gross profit rose only **2.7%** (TZS 678m to 696m). Gross margin fell **3.4 points**, from 39.4% to 36.1%.
2. **You sold fewer cartons.** Volume fell **7.3%** (220,000 to 204,000 cartons). The extra revenue came from price increases and a shift toward the bigger pack, not from selling more.
3. **One SKU did all the growth.** Tea 250g added TZS 230m, more than the whole TZS 210m increase. The 50g and 100g packs together lost 1.5% of their revenue and 10.8% of their gross profit.

## Headline numbers

| TZS m (unless stated) | 2025 | 2026 | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Net revenue | 1,720 | 1,930 | **+12.2%** |
| COGS | 1,042 | 1,234 | +18.4% |
| Gross profit | 678 | 696 | **+2.7%** |
| Gross margin | 39.4% | 36.1% | **-3.4 pts** |
| Cartons sold | 220,000 | 204,000 | -7.3% |

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Tea 50g | 120k → 100k (**-17%**) | 600 → 560 (-7%) | +12% | **+20%** | 210 → 170 (**-19%**) | 35.0% → 30.4% |

Margin fell on **all three** SKUs. The 250g pack went from 23% to 33% of revenue.

## Where the extra TZS 210m in revenue came from

| Driver | TZS m |
|---|---|
| Higher prices (5-12% per carton, depending on SKU) | +164 |
| Mix: more sales shifted to the higher-value 250g pack | +171 |
| Fewer cartons sold | -125 |
| **Total** | **+210** |

*How I split it: the volume effect values the change in total cartons at 2025's average revenue per carton. The mix effect is 2026 cartons at 2025 SKU prices, minus the volume effect. The price effect is what's left.*

## Why profit didn't follow

**Gross profit bridge:** TZS 678m in 2025, +33m from volume and mix, +164m from price, **-179m from higher cost per carton**, which gives TZS 696m in 2026.

- Cost per carton rose **15-20% on every SKU**, while prices rose 5-12%. Price increases covered only about 91% of the extra cost.
- The shift into 250g, your highest-margin pack, should have *lifted* overall margin. Rising costs wiped that out.
- If margin had held at 39.4%, gross profit would have been about **TZS 761m**, roughly **TZS 65m more** than it was.

## Check these before the board meeting

1. **Is "2026" a full year?** It's mid-September 2026. If this is a completed fiscal year (for example July 2025-June 2026), fine. If it's year-to-date, annualised or partly forecast, say so clearly. Otherwise the comparison with a full 2025 won't stand up.
2. **The 50g COGS figure is exactly the same in both years (TZS 390m), even though cartons fell 17%.** That might be fixed costs spread over fewer cartons, or a figure copied across by mistake. Check it, because it explains most of the 50g margin loss.
3. **What pushed cost per carton up 15-20%?** Possible causes include leaf prices, packaging, fuel and transport, a weaker shilling raising the cost of imported inputs, or a change in how overhead is allocated. The board will ask. The answer also tells you whether this is a one-year squeeze or a lasting problem.
4. **Cartons aren't equal across SKUs.** If every carton holds the same number of packs, the amount of tea you sold by weight actually **rose about 5%**. That would be a fairer volume measure than a 7% fall in cartons. Confirm packs per carton before quoting either figure.
5. **Where did the 250g growth come from?** New customers or channels (wholesale, HoReCa, modern trade) would be a strong story. Existing 50g and 100g buyers moving up to the bigger pack would be weaker, because then 250g is partly replacing core sales rather than adding to them. The file can't tell us which.

## A board narrative the data supports

> "Net revenue grew 12% to TZS 1.93bn, driven by pricing and strong demand for our 250g pack (+50% cartons, +58% revenue). Input costs rose faster than our prices, so gross margin fell from 39.4% to 36.1% and gross profit grew only 3%. Our entry 50g pack lost 17% of its cartons after a 12% price increase. Priorities for the coming year: recover margin through cost control and targeted pricing, protect the reach of the 50g pack, and keep building 250g."

This framing still leads with the growth. It also shows the board that you've spotted the margin problem before they do, which will do more for your credibility than a revenue-only headline.

## What I'd act on

- **Cost per carton by SKU.** Find the source of the 15-20% rise and separate what you can control from what you can't. This is the biggest lever for gross profit.
- **The 50g pack.** In Tanzania, the smallest pack is usually the entry point for price-sensitive households buying through dukas and kiosks. A 17% drop in cartons after a 12% price rise suggests these buyers are highly price-sensitive, and lost household reach is expensive to win back. Before raising the price again, consider holding a familiar shelf price or offering a smaller pack at a lower price point. (Channel data would confirm this; the file doesn't include it.)
- **The 250g pack.** Find out who is buying it and through which channels, then put more behind whatever is working. It is still your highest-margin SKU at 40%.
- **Reporting.** From now on, show gross profit and margin next to revenue in board updates, so growth is always judged against profit.
