# Kisima Water: 2027 channel priorities (provisional)

I built this from your saved Kisima context (2027-plan). That file has four long-term items and one new note on the pilot. It has no sales by channel, no margins, no budget and nothing on competitors. So this is a conditional plan to firm up in Q4 2026, not a final budget split.

## The answer

**Keep modern trade in Dar es Salaam and Arusha as the core for 2027. But don't carry the "no rural" rule into 2027 as written.**

That rule rests on one assumption: *rural consumers will not pay for branded bottled water.* The 2023, 2024 and 2025 plans all repeat it, and none of them records any evidence for it. The sales team's 5 L jar pilot is the first evidence in the file of what rural shoppers actually buy, and it points the other way. It isn't strong enough yet to pay for a rural distribution build. There were no control outlets, the figures haven't been checked, and we don't know who bought or whether the jars make money.

## What the saved context actually supports

**The "no rural" rule is an assumption, not a finding.** Repeating it in three plans is one belief copied three times, not three confirmations. The November 2025 decision against rural investment covers 2026 only and proves nothing either way. Whether to keep it for 2027 is a new decision.

**The pilot contradicts the assumption, but only within narrow limits.** The sales team reports these figures, which haven't been checked:
- 60 dukas in three districts of Morogoro Region (Morogoro Rural, Kilosa, Mvomero), over 12 weeks
- On average 38 jars per outlet per week, and 82% of outlets reordered
- Price per litre 20% below the 1.5 L bottle

If that average covers all 60 outlets for all 12 weeks, it works out to:
- about 2,280 jars a week, or roughly 27,000 jars (about 137,000 litres) over the 12 weeks
- 190 litres per outlet per week, which is 5 to 6 jars a day per shop
- about 49 of the 60 outlets reordering
- a jar costing about 2.7 times a 1.5 L bottle, so buyers paid more per purchase, not less

What the pilot can't tell us yet:

| Gap | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Does "sold through 60 dukas" mean orders from the shops or sales to shoppers? | If it counts deliveries to shops, stock sitting in shops inflates the rate |
| Did the rate hold up? We only have a 12-week average | The early buzz and extra sales-rep pushing can fade |
| Who bought? At 5 to 6 jars a day per shop, were buyers households, or eateries, institutions or other retailers restocking? | Selling to businesses is a different channel and offer from selling to rural households |
| How were the outlets chosen? Are they in district towns and trading centres or in villages? | If reps picked their best shops, the result won't predict a normal rollout. A shop in a rural district is not necessarily serving rural consumers |
| What did buyers use before: Kisima 1.5 L, a competitor, refilled or untreated water? There were no controls | This decides whether the jar grows Kisima or just moves existing litres to a price 20% lower |
| Does it make money? The file has no delivery cost, trade margin or jar margin | Rural drops may cost more than urban ones, and the jar has to cover its 20% lower price per litre |
| Did outlets reorder once, or regularly? | One reorder in 12 weeks is a low bar |

**The pilot tested something narrower than the assumption it challenges.** It tested a large value pack at a lower price per litre, in three districts of one region. It says nothing about 1.5 L or single-serve bottles at urban prices, or about other regions. One idea worth testing: in rural areas the obstacle may have been pack size and price per litre, not an unwillingness to pay for a brand. That is untested.

**The urban core has one data point, and it comes from before the 2026 push.** Kisima's 11% value share of urban modern-trade bottled water is from Q3 2025. From the file we can't tell:
- whether the 2026 focus is moving share
- how much urban bottled water sells through modern trade versus dukas and kiosks. Retail audits often miss informal outlets. If modern trade is a minority of urban volume, focusing on "urban" and focusing on "modern trade" are different choices.

**The file doesn't cover:** 2026 results, sales or margin by channel, budget, jar production capacity, competitor moves, or urban dukas and kiosks, bars and restaurants, institutional delivery or e-commerce. I haven't set priorities for those channels. If any of them carries real volume, it needs adding before the plan is final.

## Economics: break-even logic

The file has no prices, costs or margins, so I haven't given figures. Finance should fill in these two limits before January.

- **When a jar route pays:** outlets × jars per outlet per week × profit per jar (after trade margin, water, jar and delivery) must beat the weekly cost of the route. Work out the break-even jars per outlet per week, then compare it with the checked pilot rate.
- **How much the jar can take from the 1.5 L bottle:** let *m_j* be the profit per litre on the jar and *m_b* the profit per litre on the 1.5 L bottle. Let *s* be the share of jar litres that would otherwise have been bought as Kisima 1.5 L. Each jar litre adds *m_j − s × m_b*. So the jar makes money only while *m_j* is greater than *s × m_b*. *s* may be low where Kisima 1.5 L has had little presence, which is unconfirmed, and higher in peri-urban areas near current distribution.

## The go/no-go decision (rules to agree before the test starts)

- **Scale** if, in weeks 9–12, jar sales above the comparison shops stay at or above break-even, the jar makes a profit after delivery, and the 1.5 L loss stays within the limit above.
- **Adapt** if the extra demand is real but below break-even at a single duka. Try fewer, bigger outlets, delivery through wholesalers or sub-distributors, or focus on whichever area (rural or peri-urban) passes.
- **Stop** if the extra sales are close to zero, sales collapse after the first weeks, or the jar loses money.

## What we are not doing in 2027

- No regional or national rural distribution build (depots, new distributors, trucks) before the decision.
- No rural push of 1.5 L or single-serve bottles at urban prices. The pilot says nothing about them.
- No moving of urban modern-trade budget to rural before the decision.
- No treating three Morogoro Region districts as rural Tanzania. Any other region needs its own test before we invest there.

## Risks and what would change the plan

- **The pilot doesn't hold up** (stock sitting in shops, a few outlets driving the average, launch discounts): keep rural out (option A) and run only the peri-urban test.
- **Buyers are mainly businesses:** the opportunity may be supplying businesses rather than rural shoppers. Redesign the test.
- **Urban modern-trade share has fallen since Q3 2025:** find out why before committing 2027 modern-trade spend at the same level.
- **Modern trade is a small part of urban bottled-water sales:** reconsider whether urban dukas and kiosks belong in priority 1.
- **Competitors:** not assessed, because the file has no competitor information. If a competitor already sells jars in these districts, the pilot needs reading with that in mind.
