## The tightened storyline

The slide titles, read on their own, make the whole argument.

**1. We're asking the board to approve a TZS 1.8bn, two-year budget for "Nuru Cleans the Nation", paid out in two stages, each released only against agreed results.**
- On the slide: the total. First stage of TZS [X] released now. Second stage released on [date] if the agreed results are met. What the board will see at that review.
- Say: "You'll hear the case, what we know, what we don't know yet, and how this structure protects the money."

**2. Nuru gained 2.7 share points in Q2, from 18.2% to 20.9% of the urban retail audit.**
- Chart: Q1 vs Q2 share, labelled "urban retail audit".
- Only add the 30% once you've stated what it measures (value or volume sales? compared with Q1 or with Q2 last year?) and where it comes from.
- Caveat on the slide: this is one quarter. Add July–August if you have it, to show whether the gain held after the promotion ended.

**3. That gain came from a price promotion, 400 new listings and the influencer campaign running at the same time, so we can't yet say how much the campaign itself delivered.**
- Chart: a Q2 timeline showing the 6-week 15% promotion, the listings rollout and the influencer campaign dates, over weekly or monthly share or sales.
- Say: "We're showing this up front because it shapes what we're asking for."
- Moving this out of the appendix is the single biggest improvement to the deck. It makes everything after it more believable.

**4. For the next two years, the job is to hold Q2's share without discounting and turn the new listings into repeat buyers. "Nuru Cleans the Nation" is how we propose to do that, but we haven't yet tested it with Nuru buyers.**
- On the slide:
  - The job.
  - The idea in one line.
  - Why it suits a soap brand.
  - Three open questions: Do target buyers care about it? Does it change what they pick off the shelf? Would it work just as well for a rival soap brand?
- Check the job: I inferred it from the deck. If the campaign is really meant to win new buyers or support a price increase, change this title and the pilot measures on Slide 7 to match. The deck has to state its purpose one way or the other.

**5. We looked at three options. Rolling out in stages keeps the upside and limits the loss if buyers don't respond.**

| Option | Upside | Downside |
|---|---|---|
| Keep growing through price promotions and new listings | Known short-term effect | Costs margin every time and gives buyers no reason to pay full price for Nuru |
| Spend the full TZS 1.8bn now | Fastest route if the idea works | The whole budget rides on an untested idea |
| **Recommended:** pilot first, then scale up if the results justify it | Proves the idea before most of the money is spent | National scale arrives later, by the length of the pilot |

**6. The campaign pays for itself if it adds about [N] share points that promotions and new listings wouldn't have delivered.**
- On the slide:
  - Annual gross margin per share point = [category sales in the urban retail audit, TZS per year] × 1% × [Nuru gross margin %].
  - Break-even share points = TZS 1.8bn ÷ (annual gross margin per point × years of benefit).
- Show the 25% as a target, not a forecast: "Target: 25% of the urban retail audit by 2028. That needs +4.1 points on Q2, or +6.8 points if Q2's promotion-driven gain doesn't hold." Show how much of the gap you expect from the campaign and how much from more listings.

**7. The second stage is released only if pilot areas beat matched comparison areas on share at full price by [date].**
- **Pilot design:** run the campaign in [cities or outlet groups] and compare them with similar areas that don't get it. Both groups keep the same prices and promotion calendar, and you record a baseline period before the pilot starts.
- **What counts toward the release decision:**
  - Share and rate of sale at full price (the main measure).
  - Repeat purchase or the share of households buying Nuru, if you have consumer panel data.
  - Average price actually achieved.
  - Brand associations among target buyers, as a supporting check only.
- **What doesn't count:** reach and engagement. Report them, but they don't decide the release.
- **The threshold:** set it today from the Slide 6 break-even figure. If the pilot falls short, stop or redesign; don't release the second stage.
- **Risks, on the same slide:**
  - Buyers like the clean-ups but don't choose Nuru because of them.
  - A rival copies the idea or undercuts on price.
  - A soap brand cleaning rivers may prompt questions about its own packaging and ingredients, so check both before launch.
  - The share figures only cover outlets in the urban audit. [Confirm how much of Nuru's sales go through outlets the audit misses.]

**8. Decision today: approve the TZS 1.8bn budget, release the first TZS [X], and come back on [date] to decide on the second stage.**

## Before the meeting, if there's time

In order of value:

1. **Compare existing and new outlets.** Look at the Q1-to-Q2 change in outlets that stocked Nuru in both quarters, against the 400 new listings. This shows how much of the gain came from wider distribution.
2. **Compare promotion weeks with the other weeks and with the months after.** Look at share during the 6 promotion weeks against the rest of Q2, plus July–August if you have it. This shows how much of the gain the discount bought and whether it held.
3. **Find the source for the 65%, or cut it.** If it's a real study, put the country, who was surveyed, the date and the question wording on the slide.
4. **Don't try to isolate the influencer effect from Q2 data.** With three activities overlapping, it can't be done cleanly. Whatever gain is left after checks 1 and 2 is the most the campaign could have contributed, and that remainder also includes category trends and competitor moves. The pilot on Slide 7 is where you get a real answer.

Either result strengthens the storyline. If a good part of the gain held outside the promotion weeks, Slide 3 can say so and the case gets stronger. If most of it came from the discount, that sharpens Slide 4: share bought with discounts has to be bought again next quarter, and the pilot tests whether Nuru can keep it at full price.
