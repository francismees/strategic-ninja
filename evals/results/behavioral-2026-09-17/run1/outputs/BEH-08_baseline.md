**No, don't put that in the plan.** 117 million is a count of SIM subscriptions. It isn't a count of people, and it isn't a count of people you can send messages to. Used as campaign reach, it overstates the audience several times over, and anyone who knows the Tanzanian market will notice.

## Why subscriptions don't equal reach

1. **There are more subscriptions than people.** The 2022 census counted 61.7 million people, so the population today is probably around 67–70 million. More than 40% of them are under 15. So 117 million subscriptions is well over one per person, and it can't be a head count.
2. **Many people have more than one SIM.** People often keep lines on two or three networks (Vodacom, Airtel, Yas (formerly Tigo), Halotel, TTCL). They do this to get cheaper calls within a network, to use mobile money, or for better coverage. So one person can show up as two or three subscriptions.
3. **Some subscriptions aren't in anyone's phone.** The total can include data-only SIMs in MiFi devices and routers, SIMs in machines like card terminals and trackers, and lines that still count as active but are rarely used.
4. **You can only message numbers you have access to.** Your channel sets your reach, not the size of the national market. That means either your own list of people who agreed to hear from you, or the customers a mobile network or bulk SMS provider will message for you. No single provider covers every network's full customer base.
5. **You need consent.** Promotional bulk SMS normally has to go out under a registered sender name through a licensed provider. Tanzania's Personal Data Protection Act 2022 and TCRA's consumer protection rules also limit marketing messages to people who haven't consented. So messaging "everyone" isn't legally possible anyway. Check the current rules with your SMS provider and your lawyer.

## A realistic ceiling

My estimate is that **roughly 30–40 million individual people** in Tanzania use a mobile phone. That's my estimate, not an official figure. It's the most any SMS campaign could reach, before you narrow by audience, consent and budget. Check it against GSMA Intelligence's count of unique mobile subscribers or FinScope Tanzania before you quote it.

## What to put in the plan instead

**Market context** (fine to cite, but check the exact TCRA quarter and which category the figure covers):

> "Tanzania has ~117 million mobile subscriptions (TCRA, [quarter/year]). Because many people use more than one SIM, this represents an estimated 30–40 million individual mobile users."

**Campaign reach** (the number that actually matters):

> "Planned reach: [N] numbers we have permission to message via [own database / operator targeted SMS / aggregator]. Expected messages delivered: [N × delivery rate]."

Work the reach number out step by step:

| Step | Where the number comes from |
|---|---|
| Numbers you can target | The size of your own opt-in list, or the audience size an operator or provider quotes for your targeting |
| Remove duplicates | If you buy from several operators, the same person may be counted on two or more networks |
| × Delivery rate | Use the provider's delivery reports. Don't assume 100% |
| **= Delivered reach** | **This is your reach number** |
| × Response rate | Use your own past campaigns or a test send. SMS response rates vary a lot depending on the offer |

*Made-up example:* an operator offers to message 2,000,000 of its subscribers in Dar es Salaam, aged 25–45, who have agreed to receive promotions. At an assumed 90% delivery rate, reach is about 1.8 million, not 117 million.

**Before you lock the numbers,** do a small test send (10,000–50,000 messages) through the provider you plan to use. That gives you real delivery and response rates to base the plan on.
