# The five segments aren't ready for personas yet

I haven't written the personas, and I wouldn't use these five segments for next year's targeting. Your analyst's notes show three problems: the segments change when the model is rerun, five segments don't show up again in a split-half test, and the segments don't buy any differently from each other. Personas would make them look solid when they aren't. Below I set out the problems, what you can use in the meantime, and what the rerun needs to produce segments worth writing up.

## What the output shows

| Issue | What the file says | Why it matters for targeting |
|---|---|---|
| **People switch segments between runs** | Adjusted Rand index of 0.31–0.44 across random seeds | Many respondents land in a different segment just because the algorithm started from a different point. You'd normally want about 0.7 or higher before treating clusters as real. A persona would describe one random run. |
| **Five segments don't hold up** | Split-half replication found four clusters | At least one of the five probably exists only because the model was told to find five. This output can't show which one. |
| **The segments don't buy differently** | No significant difference in how often they buy or which brand they bought last | This is the biggest problem. If the segments buy equally often and choose the same brands, shifting spend from one to another won't change who buys or how much. The "Brand-loyal" group's last purchases look like everyone else's, so it isn't a group of loyal buyers for any particular brand. |
| **Cluster 5 is too small** | n = 55 (9% of the sample) | Every percentage for this group has a margin of error of about ±13 points. Spread across three cities, that's fewer than 20 people per city on average. It's too thin for a persona or a media plan. |
| **No profile data** | Only segment sizes and attitude labels | Age, income, city, where people shop and what media they use aren't in the file. Any persona written now would be made up. |

## What you can use for next year's plan now

**1. Target by what people buy, not by these attitudes.** The survey already asked how often people buy and which brand they bought last. That gives you a simple setup that can be trusted and is tied to revenue:

- **Our buyers:** keep them and grow how much they buy
- **Competitor buyers:** win them over
- **Light or occasional buyers of the category:** get them buying more often

You can size all three from the same 640 responses, and split them by city if the numbers are big enough.

**2. Test the five attitude themes as messages, not audiences.** The themes still work as creative angles. Run them as message versions aimed at the groups above and see which one wins with each group. If one theme clearly wins with a particular group, that's real evidence of a segment, which this survey hasn't provided.

|---|---|---|---|
| Value | Thamani | Price-focused | Price per use, pack sizes, promotions |
| Wellness | Afya | Health-conscious | Health benefits, what's in it and what isn't |

| Discovery | Ugunduzi | Adventurous | New variants, limited editions, trying something new |

These names are for the message angles only. Hold off on final segment names until you know the segments are real, because names tend to stick inside an organisation once they're in use.

## What the rerun needs before personas

Share this with the analyst:

- **Rule out answering habits first.** With agree/disagree questions, clusters often group people by how they answer (agreeing with everything, or always picking the middle) rather than by what they believe. That would explain this exact pattern: segments that shift between runs and don't match behaviour. Adjust each person's answers against their own average, or use trade-off questions (MaxDiff) in the next survey.
- **Compare solutions with 3 to 6 segments.** Pick the one that holds across random seeds and in both halves of the sample, not just the one with the best fit statistic.
- **Tie the segments to behaviour.** Build them from needs and buying behaviour together, or at least reject any solution whose segments don't differ on something that matters commercially.
- **Agree the pass mark before rerunning:**
  - Adjusted Rand index of about 0.7 or higher across seeds
  - The same number of segments in both halves of the sample
  - Significant differences in at least one behaviour you care about (how often people buy, which brand, how much they spend)
  - At least about 100 respondents in every segment, with extra sample for any small group that matters strategically. For example, if status seekers are a premium opportunity, recruit more of them.
- **Profile each segment:** age, gender, income, city, where they shop (duka, supermarket, open market, online), brands used, media and social platforms, and whether they prefer ads in Swahili or English.

Also note that the sample covers only urban adults in Dar es Salaam, Arusha and Mwanza. If next year's plan includes other towns or rural areas, these results can't tell you about those people.

Once a solution meets that pass mark, send me the profiles for each segment and I'll write the full personas and final names.
