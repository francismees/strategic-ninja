## Short answer: these interviews can't give you a reliable percentage

The file doesn't support a "% of Tanzanian parents" figure, and I'd keep a percentage off the slide. There are four reasons:

1. **The file says it's made up.** The header reads "Invented for evaluation" and calls itself a "synthetic fixture." If that's accurate, none of these are real parents. Nothing from it should go into a client or investor deck as real research, including the quotes. If you have the real transcripts, send them and I'll redo this.
2. **Six people aren't a sample of "Tanzanian parents."** All six have a child in a private primary school, and they're from Dodoma and Dar only. That leaves out public-school parents, rural households and every other region. With n=6, each person moves the number by about 17 points.
3. **Most of them didn't state a preference.** They mostly describe what they do now, which isn't the same as what they'd choose. Only one person says "I prefer," and he means paying in person.
4. **The translations haven't been checked.** The moderator translated them and nobody else has verified them.

### How each parent answered

| Parent | What they said | Pays by mobile money? | Stated preference |
|---|---|---|---|
| P1, mother, Dodoma | Switched from bank to phone, but the school wants a bank receipt | Yes | None stated |
| P2, father, Dar | Phone is easy, "but the charges..." (the moderator didn't follow up) | Implied | Mixed, unclear |
| P3, mother, Dar | Pays in small monthly amounts by phone | Yes | None stated |
| P4, father, Dodoma | Prefers paying at the school so he can see it happen | No | **In person** |
| P5, mother, Dar | Uses the phone if she has balance, otherwise waits | Only when she has funds | Depends on cash |
| P6, grandmother, Dodoma | Her grandchild helps her send it | Probably, with help | None stated |

Depending on how you count, the answer runs from **0% who explicitly prefer mobile money** to **about 50–80% who use it at least some of the time**. That spread is why a single number would mislead.

**What you can honestly say:** in 6 interviews, most parents already pay fees by phone at least some of the time. But it isn't smooth yet. They mention charges, schools asking for bank receipts, not having enough balance, needing someone's help, and wanting to see the payment made.

If you show counts, write them as counts with n, not as percentages. For example: "3 of 6 described paying by phone; 1 of 6 preferred paying in person (n=6, qualitative)."

### Three quotes for the deck

I chose a balanced set: the shift to mobile money, the opportunity, and the pushback. Three quotes that all favour mobile money would suggest a preference the interviews don't show.

1. **The shift, and a school-side barrier.** P1, mother, Dodoma
   > "Last time I took the money to the bank, but now I pay by phone. But the school wants a bank receipt."
   > *"Nilipeleka pesa benki mara ya mwisho, lakini sasa nalipa kwa simu. Ila shule inataka risiti ya benki."*

2. **Paying in instalments.** P3, mother, Dar es Salaam
   > "I pay little by little every month by phone."
   > *"Mimi nalipa kidogo kidogo kila mwezi kwa simu."*

3. **Trust.** P4, father, Dodoma
   > "I prefer to pay at the school myself so that I can see."
   > *"Napendelea kulipa mwenyewe shuleni ili nione."*

**Backups:** P5 ("If I have balance, phone. If not, I wait.") is good for cash-flow timing. Use P2 ("Phone is easy. But the charges...") carefully: the moderator didn't follow up, so don't present it as proof that fees are the barrier.

**Before these go on a slide:**
- Confirm that this is real research. The file is labelled synthetic.
- Get a second Kiswahili speaker to check the translations, especially *nione* ("so that I can see") and *salio* ("balance," which could mean wallet balance or cash in general).
- Put the Kiswahili next to the English. It adds credibility with a Tanzanian audience.
- Label speakers by role and city only, and make sure participants agreed to be quoted.

### If you need a real percentage

Run a short survey of about 380–400 parents. That gets you roughly ±5 points. Split it across public and private schools and urban and rural areas. Ask two separate questions:
- **What they do:** "How did you pay last term's fees?"
- **What they'd choose:** "If the school accepted all options at the same cost, which would you use?"

Add one question each on charges and receipts, since those were the barriers that came up here.
