# Fizzi Cola 2027 brand and marketing plan: win back the on-the-go buyer

> **Synthetic demonstration.** Fizzi Beverages, Bora Cola, all data, respondents and quotations are fictional. The only real figure used is the NBS Mainland CPI for August 2026, cited as context. This document shows the output of [Workflow B](../../workflows/annual-strategy.md) at standard depth.

## 1. Decision and recommendation

**Decision requested:** approve Phase 1 of the on-the-go plan: 180 coolers with matched controls, measurement of which brand comes to mind at the moment, and communications in the cooler clusters. A gate review follows at the end of Q2 2027. Also stop the planned shift of 40% of trade budget to modern trade.

**Recommendation (conditional):** Fizzi is losing on-the-go buyers at the moment of purchase, wherever cold Fizzi is missing. Win that moment back with chilled 350 ml availability in high-footfall clusters, on a repaired supply base. Scale only if Phase 1 shows the pilot uplift holds.

## 2. What happened and why

- **Growth came from frequency, while buyers fell.** In Dar es Salaam, the share of households buying Fizzi fell from 36% to 34%. Buyers bought more often (13 occasions a year versus 12), so volume still grew 2.3%. Fewer buyers took away about 330,000 litres; more frequent buying added about 462,000. In Mwanza, penetration fell from 34% to 30% and volume fell 7%. That loss overlaps the H1 distributor problem ([Demo A](../demo-a-diagnosis/diagnosis-memo.md)).
- **The lost buyers are 350 ml buyers.** In Dar es Salaam, 350 ml household penetration fell from 21.7% to 18.4% (−12% bottles). The 1.25 L home pack grew 22%. The household panel under-records out-of-home purchases, so the true on-the-go loss is uncertain and may be larger.
- **The mechanism: the cold bottle wins the moment.** In a matched pilot, 22 outlets that received coolers sold about 21 more litres of 350 ml a week than expected, a 49% uplift; 500 ml barely moved. In ten short interviews at bus stages and a market street, six people said cold availability decides the purchase, and none raised price. One Dar es Salaam commuter (R02) said *"Fizzi ilikuwa ya moto, nikachukua hii"* ("Fizzi was warm, so I took this one"; analyst gloss).
- **A competitor is ahead at the moment.** Route supervisors estimate that Bora Cola placed coolers in 1,500–2,500 Dar es Salaam dukas near transport stages in 2025. This is an estimate; we have no competitor sales data.

**What this is not:** a price problem (value perception is stable at 57–58%, and price did not come up in interviews), or a loyalty problem (frequency is rising).

## 3. Where growth will come from

The 2027 growth task is **recruiting back on-the-go 350 ml buyers in Dar es Salaam**, then in Mwanza once supply is repaired. Lever: chilled physical availability, supported by being the brand that comes to mind for "a cold drink on the way home". The tracker does not yet measure this, so we will add it.

## 5. Choices and non-choices

- **Where to play:** high-footfall dukas and kiosks near Dar es Salaam transport stages first; Mwanza clusters once distribution is back to within 3 points of 2025 levels.
- **How to win:** be the cold bottle within reach at the moment of need, and the brand that comes to mind for it.
- **We will not:** launch a premium tier, move 40% of trade budget to modern trade, sponsor a football club, or roll out coolers nationally before the Phase 1 gate.

## 7. Economics

- **Known:** budget TZS 3,200m (flat). Cooler cost TZS 1.6m installed plus TZS 0.25m a year. 350 ml contribution about TZS 700 per litre (internal estimate). Phase 1 commitment TZS 288m plus TZS 45m a year.
- **At pilot uplift** (about 21 incremental litres a week), a cooler pays back in **about 3.1 years**. A **two-year payback needs about 29 incremental litres a week**, which at the same relative uplift means outlets with a 350 ml baseline near 59 litres a week ([calculation](analysis/cooler_economics.md)).
- **Implication:** the programme works only if it targets high-baseline outlets and the uplift replicates. The gate (at least 25 incremental litres a week and 90% uptime) protects against a value-destroying roll-out. How many outlets meet the baseline threshold is not yet known; ranking outlets by sales is a Phase 1 dependency.
- **Context, not cost evidence:** Mainland household transport prices rose 13.8% in the year to August 2026 (NBS). That does not measure Fizzi's distribution costs; finance should check cooler servicing and delivery costs directly.

## 9. Measurement and learning

- **Delivery (not success):** reach of cluster communications.
- **Behaviour (leading):** 350 ml litres per outlet per week, cooler outlets vs matched controls; cooler uptime.
- **Brand effect:** situational retrieval in cooler clusters vs other areas (baseline Q4 2026).
- **Business outcome (lagging):** household penetration in Dar es Salaam (at least 34% in 2027; stretch 35% if Phase 2 proceeds) and Mwanza; volume and contribution.
- **Gate at end of Q2 2027:** scale, adapt or stop by the rules above.

## How this plan was checked

A distinct self-review against the red-team gates (not an independent reviewer). Open concerns:
- The consumer mechanism rests partly on a small qualitative base.
- The cooler payback is marginal at pilot baselines.
- The competitor response has not been assessed.

One FAIL was repaired: the Kenyan proxy used for a pack launch. Passing these checks does not prove the plan will work; Phase 1 is designed to find out.
