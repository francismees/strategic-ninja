# Insight calibration examples

> **All four examples are synthetic teaching fixtures.** Brands, figures, respondents and quotations were invented for this file to show reasoning. They are not real market facts and must not be cited as evidence. Any resemblance to real brands is coincidental.

Method: [../references/insight.md](../references/insight.md). Each example shows the evidence, the inference boundary and the resulting decision.

---

## 1. An observation mislabelled as insight

**Claimed insight (from a draft plan):** "Young urban Tanzanians are digital natives who live on social media, so our brand must be social-first."

**What the evidence actually is:** a general statement with no source, no contrast and no link to category behaviour.

**Diagnosis of the defect**
- It is at best an *observation* about media use, and as written it is unsourced.
- No contrast: social media use compared with what (other media, other age groups, category buyers vs non-buyers)?
- No mechanism: nothing explains why this age group buys or does not buy the brand.
- "Digital natives" is a demographic label used as an explanation.
- It cannot change a decision responsibly: "social-first" could be right or wrong depending on reach among category buyers, cost per reached buyer and the brand's actual constraint.

**What would be needed to turn it into something usable**
- Reach data for the brand's category buyers by channel (unique people reached, not followers or subscriptions).
- Evidence on the brand's constraint (if the constraint is availability, media mix is secondary).

**Inference boundary**
- *Can conclude*: nothing about the brand's strategy yet.
- *Cannot conclude*: that social channels reach the brand's buyers efficiently, or that younger buyers are the growth source.
- *Smallest next step*: pull platform unique-reach estimates for the target region and age range and compare with radio and outdoor reach among category buyers.

**Decision:** do not adopt "social-first" on this basis. Label the statement as an unsourced observation; keep channel choice open until reach and constraint evidence exist.

## 2. An appealing but unsupported psychological story

**Claimed insight:** "Mothers buy the 1.25 L bottle because sharing one bottle symbolises family unity. Our campaign should celebrate togetherness."

**Evidence supplied (synthetic)**
- E1: 1.25 L sales are 2.4 times higher on Saturdays and Sundays than on weekdays (sell-out data, 120 outlets, Jan–Jun).
- E2: 1.25 L is 18% cheaper per litre than two 500 ml bottles at shelf (price checks, 40 outlets).
- E3: No qualitative research on why shoppers choose 1.25 L.

**Assessment**
- Observation (E1) and contrast (weekend vs weekday) are supported.
- The mechanism "sharing symbolises family unity" is **invented psychology**: nothing in the evidence addresses meaning or symbolism.
- Competing mechanisms fit the same pattern at least as well: weekend family meals need more volume (occasion size); lower price per litre (value); weekend shopping trips from home rather than from work (shopper mission).
- The togetherness story might be true, but it is not established and it bridges an evidence gap.

**Status:** mechanism hypothesis (competing with occasion-size and value explanations).

**Inference boundary**
- *Can conclude*: 1.25 L is a weekend pack and is better value per litre.
- *Cannot conclude*: why shoppers choose it, or that a togetherness message would increase sales.
- *Smallest next step*: eight to twelve short intercept interviews with weekend 1.25 L buyers, plus a check of whether weekday 1.25 L sales rise when price per litre gaps widen during promotions.

**Decision:** do not brief a "togetherness" campaign on this basis. If a weekend-occasion campaign is considered, frame the communications task around the supported facts (weekend meal occasion, value per litre) and test the emotional territory in research.

## 3. An evidence-backed consumer mechanism

**Question:** Why does the 350 ml pack underperform in some duka clusters?

**Evidence (synthetic)**
- E1 (sell-out, 260 outlets): 350 ml sells 2.1 times more per week in outlets with a working cooler than in outlets without; for 500 ml the ratio is 1.2.
- E2 (before/after, 22 outlets that received a cooler in March, with 22 matched outlets that did not): 350 ml weekly sales rose 58% in cooler outlets versus 6% in matched outlets over the following ten weeks; 500 ml rose 9% versus 4%.
- E3 (14 commuter interviews at two Dar es Salaam bus stages, fixture IDs R01–R14): 9 of 14 describe buying a small drink "right now" when they get off the bus; 6 of 14 say they skip buying when nothing cold is available. Example quote (R07, original Kiswahili, analyst translation): *"Kama hakuna baridi, naacha tu"* — "If there's nothing cold, I just skip it."
- E4 (sales by hour, 30 outlets with EPOS): 350 ml peaks between 16:00 and 19:00; 500 ml is flatter across the day.

**Chain**
- Observation: 350 ml sales are much higher where coolers work.
- Contrast: cooler vs non-cooler outlets; 350 ml vs 500 ml; before vs after installation with matched controls.
- Mechanism: the 350 ml pack serves an immediate-consumption occasion (e.g. after the commute) in which a chilled product is the deciding condition; without cold availability the purchase is often skipped rather than switched to a warm pack.
- Alternatives considered: (a) coolers go to busier outlets (selection) — weakened by the matched before/after comparison; (b) coolers come with extra visibility or merchandising — plausible contributor; cannot be separated with this data; (c) seasonality — matched controls rose only 6%.
- Tension (optional, evidenced here): people want an immediate cold drink, and the category often offers only warm stock at the moment of need.

**Insight (defensible, medium confidence):** For 350 ml, chilled availability at the moment of immediate consumption, not brand preference, is the main driver of whether a purchase happens in these duka clusters.

**Inference boundary**
- *Can conclude*: in the observed clusters and period, cooler presence is strongly associated with, and very likely contributes to, higher 350 ml sales.
- *Cannot conclude*: the effect size in other regions or rural outlets; how much comes from visibility rather than chilling; how many buyers this represents in the population (interview counts describe the sample only).
- *Smallest next step*: extend the matched rollout to two more regions with a cooler-only vs cooler-plus-merchandising split.

**Decision:** prioritise cooler placement at high-footfall duka clusters near transport stages for 350 ml, with a staged rollout and matched controls, before increasing 350 ml advertising.

## 4. An operational and commercial insight with no human tension

**Question:** Why did revenue per litre fall in the first half even though list prices rose 5%?

**Evidence (synthetic)**
- E1 (`scripts/commercial_analysis.py pvm`, SKU × channel grain): revenue +2%; price effect +4.1%; volume effect +3.0%; mix effect −5.1%.
- E2 (contribution analysis): the 1.25 L pack's share of wholesale-channel volume rose from 22% to 35%.
- E3 (trade promotion calendar): a wholesaler volume rebate on 1.25 L ran February–May.
- E4 (distributor invoices): wholesaler 1.25 L purchases spiked in the rebate months; sell-out data from 40 downstream dukas show only a modest 1.25 L increase.

**Chain**
- Observation: negative mix offset most of the price increase.
- Contrast: wholesale vs other channels; rebate months vs other months; sell-in vs downstream sell-out.
- Mechanism: the rebate shifted wholesaler buying towards the lowest price-per-litre pack; much of the extra 1.25 L volume was loaded into wholesaler stock rather than sold through.
- Alternatives: genuine shopper trade-down to large packs (weakened by the modest downstream sell-out increase); a data classification change (checked: SKU codes unchanged).
- No consumer tension is involved, and none is needed.

**Insight (defensible, medium-high confidence):** The rebate bought wholesaler stock of the lowest-margin pack rather than shopper demand, diluting revenue per litre and creating a likely sell-in hangover.

**Inference boundary**
- *Can conclude*: the mix decline is linked to the rebate's effect on wholesaler buying.
- *Cannot conclude*: the full size of the stock build without wholesaler inventory data; the rebate's effect on market share.
- *Smallest next step*: obtain wholesaler stock counts for the top 20 wholesalers.

**Decision:** redesign the rebate (tie it to downstream sell-out or balanced pack mix), expect lower 1.25 L sell-in next quarter as stock clears, and do not read that dip as a demand collapse.
