# Calibration cases: counterexamples and boundary conditions

> **Fictional teaching cases.** Every case below is invented to calibrate judgement. None is a verified historical case, and none should be cited as evidence that a strategy works. Verified historical precedents are deferred to a later release (they need sourcing and scope checks); when added, they will be kept in a separate, clearly labelled section. A precedent is an analogy to examine, not proof that a strategy transfers.

Use with [../references/red-team.md](../references/red-team.md) to avoid both failure directions: passing weak work, and rejecting sound work.

## 1. The simple fix is the right answer
**Situation:** A snack brand's volume fell 9%. Out-of-stock rates in its top 300 outlets rose from 6% to 21% after a warehouse move; rate of sale where stocked is unchanged.
**Wrong response:** "This lacks a compelling consumer insight; let's research the brand's emotional territory."
**Calibrated response:** Diagnosis supported: availability. Recommend restoring supply with service-level targets and weekly out-of-stock tracking. No novelty is required. Brand research can wait.

## 2. An action competitors could copy is still correct
**Situation:** Competitors could also add van sales to peri-urban dukas. The client's decline is concentrated in exactly those outlets, and pilot routes paid back within a quarter.
**Wrong response:** "Fails the competitor-substitution test: not differentiating."
**Calibrated response:** The substitution test applies to positioning and advantage claims, not to operational actions that fix the diagnosed constraint. Recommend scaling van sales with payback tracking; consider how to sustain service quality as competitors respond.

## 3. Retention is the binding constraint
**Situation:** A home water-delivery subscription grows sign-ups 15% a month but loses 45% of new customers by month two; acquisition costs are rising.
**Wrong response:** "Brands grow by penetration; increase acquisition spend."
**Calibrated response:** Early churn destroys the value of acquisition. Diagnose churn timing and reasons (delivery reliability, deposit terms, price per litre), fix onboarding and service, and show the unit economics at different retention rates. Acquisition remains essential, but retention is the current constraint.

## 4. The competitor's tactic that does not transfer
**Situation:** A competitor's football sponsorship coincided with a share gain. The proposal: "Sponsor a rival club."
**Transfer test:** The competitor also doubled cooler placements and cut its 500 ml price during the same season (the mechanism is unclear); the client lacks the distribution to convert extra fame into sales; the second sponsor would get less attention.
**Calibrated response:** Do not copy the sponsorship on this evidence. Investigate where the competitor's gain came from (outlets, packs, regions). If availability explains it, match the mechanism, not the tactic.

## 5. Premium strategy without willingness-to-pay evidence
**Situation:** "Consumers are trading up globally; launch a premium juice at +40%."
**Calibrated response:** Global trend articles are global hypotheses. Check whether premium tiers already sell in the target outlets, the price ladder, cannibalisation risk and margin logic. Recommendation type: research/experiment first — a priced limited-outlet pilot with matched controls and a pre-agreed rate-of-sale threshold.

## 6. Clusters that do not survive
**Situation:** A survey segmentation produced six named personas. Re-running the clustering on a random half of the sample produces a different four-cluster solution; two personas have bases under 60.
**Calibrated response:** Do not name these as durable target audiences. Report the instability, segment on behaviours that matter to the decision (purchase occasion, pack, outlet type), and retest on fresh data before investing.

## 7. Strong reach, no behaviour change
**Situation:** A campaign reached a large share of the target audience with high recall. Trial, rate of sale and brand retrieval in buying situations did not move versus control regions.
**Wrong response:** "The campaign was a success: reach and recall beat benchmarks."
**Calibrated response:** Exposure is not response. Locate the failed link: was the product available where people were reached; was the brand linked to the message; did the proposition address a real barrier; was price the constraint? Decide change or stop by the pre-agreed rule.

## 8. Excessive caution
**Situation:** The user asks, "Quick gut check: should we prioritise Mwanza or Arusha next year?" and supplies a one-page regional summary.
**Wrong response:** Ten clarifying questions and no view.
**Calibrated response:** A provisional view from the summary (e.g. Mwanza, because the category is larger and the brand's distribution gap is wider there), the main assumption behind it, what would reverse it, and the one or two numbers worth checking.

## 9. A workshop that should not conclude
**Situation:** A leadership workshop has conflicting evidence on whether modern trade is growing in the category.
**Wrong response:** A storyline stating "Modern trade is the future" as the agreed conclusion.
**Calibrated response:** Present the evidence and its scope differences, the choices that depend on the answer, and the audit data or test needed. Record the open question and the decision date.

## 10. A correct objection to a "viral" brief
**Situation:** The CEO asks for "a viral TikTok campaign to fix sales".
**Calibrated response:** Acknowledge the ambition, then reframe: which business outcome and which behaviour must change, for whom, what barrier stops it (is it communication at all?), and what the communications task is. Virality may be a distribution mechanism for a message; it is not a strategy or an objective. Offer a short plan to establish the task before briefing creative.
