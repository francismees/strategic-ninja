# Fizzi Cola H1 2026: what is really driving the decline

> **Synthetic demonstration.** Fizzi Beverages, its data and its people are fictional. This memo shows the output of [Workflow A](../../workflows/diagnose.md) at standard depth on the files in [data/](data/).

**To:** CEO, Fizzi Beverages · **Re:** your request for a new campaign brief · **Depth:** standard

## The answer

Fizzi is not mainly losing demand. It is losing shelves. About nine-tenths of the H1 volume decline sits in wholesaler-served dukas in Mwanza and Arusha. After the January switch to Distributor B, the share of those outlets stocking Fizzi fell by about 20 points and out-of-stocks roughly tripled. Where Fizzi was on shelf, it appears to have sold at the same rate as before.

**Recommendation:** restore availability in those two regions first and hold the new-campaign brief. A new campaign now would spend money persuading people to buy a product that has disappeared from roughly a third of the wholesaler-served dukas that used to stock it in the regions where sales fell. Media weight may still matter for the longer term. Test that separately once availability is back.

## What happened

- **Net sales fell 6.1%** (TZS 6,215m to 5,838m). **Volume fell 8.9%** (3,315 to 3,021 thousand litres). The 5% increase on the 500 ml pack added about TZS 167m and lower volume removed about TZS 551m; mix was negligible. So price rises masked a larger volume problem.
- **The decline is concentrated.** Wholesaler-served dukas lost 36% of volume in Mwanza and 31% in Arusha, together 266 of the 294 thousand litres lost. Every other region and channel moved between −2% and +1%.
- **Availability collapsed exactly there.** Field audits show distribution in those outlets fell from 58% to 37% (Mwanza) and 55% to 38% (Arusha), with out-of-stocks up from about 11–12% to 31–34%. Other duka channels moved by a point or less.
- **Where stocked, sales held up.** Volume per point of distribution was essentially unchanged in both regions. This is a rough proxy (sell-in divided by an audit sample), so treat it as supporting, not conclusive.

## Why not advertising?

Your concern is reasonable. Ad recall fell five points after this year's 20% cut in TV and radio weight. But three things point away from advertising as the main cause:

1. **The media cut was uniform across regions; the losses were not.** Dar es Salaam and Dodoma saw the same weight cut and held volume.
2. **Consideration held steady** (52% to 51%) and awareness is unchanged.
3. **Claimed purchase fell only in the two regions that lost availability** (Mwanza 37% to 29%, Arusha 36% to 30%; Dar es Salaam unchanged at 40%). The regional survey bases are small (about 150), so this is directional.

Lower media weight could still erode the brand over time. That is a separate question with a separate test (below).

## Other explanations we checked

| Explanation | Verdict | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Category-wide decline | Unlikely | Most regions and channels are flat; no category data supplied |
| 500 ml price gap | Open, minor | In Dar es Salaam, Fizzi 500 ml went from 3% to 9% above the main competitor; 500 ml dipped slightly in unaffected groups. Price checks exist only for Dar es Salaam |
| Wholesaler destocking | Open, affects size of recovery | Sell-in can fall faster than outlet sales if wholesalers cut stock. Outlet out-of-stocks rose, so destocking is not the whole story |
| Local competitor push in Mwanza and Arusha | Not tested | No competitor data for those regions |

## What we recommend

| Action | Owner | When | How we will know |
|---|---|---|---|
| Agree weekly delivery and a fill-rate target with Distributor B for Mwanza and Arusha wholesalers | Regional sales manager, Lake and Northern zones | Weeks 1–4 | Delivery frequency and fill rate (not measured today; start now) |
| If weekly service is not in place by week 6, run temporary direct van sales to top duka clusters | Head of route-to-market | Weeks 6–18 if triggered | Distribution and out-of-stocks in the monthly audit; route contribution after 8 weeks |
| Hold the new-campaign brief; keep current creative at approved weight | Marketing director | H2 2026, review end of Q3 | Tracker consideration and regional claimed purchase |
| Check 500 ml price gaps in all four regions before any price decision | Insights manager | Weeks 2–8 | Rate of sale by price-gap band |
| Once availability recovers, test restoring media weight in one stable region versus a comparison region for 12 weeks | Marketing director and insights manager | After Q3 review | Consideration, claimed purchase and volume against pre-period trends |

**What we are not doing:** no new campaign creative in H2 until availability recovers, and no national price change based on Dar es Salaam checks alone.

**What is at stake:** about 266 thousand litres per half-year, roughly TZS 500m of net sales at current prices. The cost of fixing distributor service or running van routes is not yet known. Get quotes before committing beyond week 6.

**Success test:** distribution in the affected outlets back to within 3 points of 2025 levels by end of Q4. Volume in those groups (now at 66% of H1 2025) should close to within 5 points of the comparison groups (now at 98%) by H1 2027.

**What would change our mind:** if availability recovers and volume in Mwanza and Arusha is still more than 10% behind the comparison groups, reopen the diagnosis. Look again at communications, competition and category.

## Confidence and limits

- **Medium confidence** in the diagnosis. Several independent first-party signals (sales, audits, tracker) point the same way. The limits: the sales data are sell-in, the rate-of-sale proxy is rough, and the regional survey bases are small.
- The delivery-frequency explanation rests on one sales email. Measuring delivery frequency is the first action.
- This was a **self-review** against the skill's red-team standards, not an independent review. Open concerns: sell-in data, the rate-of-sale proxy, and unknown costs of fixing distribution.
