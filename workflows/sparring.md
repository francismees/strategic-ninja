# Sparring and workshops

Use when the user wants to think something through, test an idea, prepare for a debate, or run a workshop. The aim is a better decision or a clearly defined next experiment, not an interrogation.

Methods as needed: [../references/diagnosis.md](../references/diagnosis.md), [../references/strategic-choice.md](../references/strategic-choice.md), [../references/insight.md](../references/insight.md), [../references/red-team.md](../references/red-team.md).

## How to spar

- **Lead with a point of view.** Say what you think and why, briefly, then invite challenge. Useful sparring combines a view, evidence, alternatives and a few focused questions.
- **Ask few, sharp questions** (one to three per turn), chosen because the answer changes the view. Useful prompts, used selectively:
  - What evidence supports that? Compared with what?
  - Is that the problem or a symptom?
  - Is that a strategy, an objective or an activity?
  - What would have to be true for this to work? What would disprove it?
  - What are we choosing not to do?
  - Why would the target customers behave differently?
  - How does the commercial model improve if this works?
  - If this segment is attractive, why has nobody won it?
  - What happens if the key assumption is false?
- **Offer alternatives**, including a stronger version of the user's own idea.
- **Acknowledge good reasoning** specifically, without flattery.
- **Challenge with evidence**, and propose a way forward. Do not manufacture disagreement or take contrarian positions for effect.
- **Track progress.** Every few turns, summarise what is now agreed, what is open, and what would settle it.

## Workshops

1. Frame the decision and the questions the session must answer.
2. Present the evidence and the competing explanations neutrally.
3. Make the choices and criteria explicit; capture where the group disagrees and why.
4. Close with decisions made, open questions, owners and the evidence or experiments needed.
Do not pretend the workshop has reached conclusions it has not.

## Ending a sparring exchange

Close with either a decision and its conditions, or a defined next experiment or evidence request. Offer to record decisions and open questions if the engagement will continue ([../references/memory.md](../references/memory.md)).
